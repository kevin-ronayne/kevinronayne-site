SOCIAL & RELATIONAL WELL-BEING

Understand your bird’s world.

Birds are intriguing creatures, from the tiny Cuban bee hummingbird to the much larger ostrich. Each species has unique needs, particularly when it comes to companionship, activity, and well-being.

Changes in a bird’s environment, the loss of a companion, boredom, or illness can affect behavior. Pay attention to what is normal for your bird and contact an avian veterinarian when you notice concerning changes.

Changes worth noticing

The Paradigm care guide highlights several possible signs of distress. These signs do not establish a diagnosis; they are reasons to pay closer attention and seek professional advice.

◔

Reduced appetite

Your bird eats less than usual or loses interest in familiar food.

✧

Increased irritability

Interactions that were once comfortable seem to upset your bird.

↗

Aggressive behavior

You notice unusual defensiveness or other changes in behavior.

♫

Changes in song

Vocalizations sound different or become less frequent than normal.

Support connection and well-being

Learn your bird’s usual behaviors, offer appropriate enrichment and social interaction for its species, and watch for changes in appetite, mood, or vocalizations. Relational needs differ between birds, so individualized advice matters.

When something changes, reach out.

Tell your veterinarian about changes in appetite or behavior. Early evaluation can help identify health concerns and guide appropriate care.

Not sure what your bird needs?

Our Pexperts can discuss your bird’s daily routine and help you think through questions about social needs and care. For symptoms or suspected illness, contact an avian veterinarian.
