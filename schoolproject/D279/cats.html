FEEDING THROUGH THE YEARS

Care that grows with your cat.

Cats were first domesticated around 7500 BCE in western Asia. With more than 60 documented breeds, feeding needs can differ by age, breed, diet, and health. Use these life stages as a starting point and consult your veterinarian for advice tailored to your cat.

STAGE 01

Zero to four weeks

If possible, the mother cat (or “queen”) should nurse her kittens directly. Monitor growth closely and contact a veterinarian if a kitten is not gaining weight steadily.

Reasons for insufficient growth can include competition for milk, gastrointestinal disease, and environmental conditions such as extreme heat, cold, or unsanitary surroundings. Bottle or tube feeding should be discussed with a veterinarian.

STAGE 02

Four weeks to one year

Kittens may begin transitioning to soft wet kitten food at about three to four weeks of age. Kitten-formulated food provides nutrients for growth, energy, and wellness. A small amount of water may soften hard food when appropriate.

The supplied care guide cites ASPCA feeding quantities, but the right amount depends on the kitten and the food. Follow the food label and ask your veterinarian for an individualized recommendation.

What to look for

Food formulated for kittens, an appropriate feeding plan, and steady growth as your kitten develops.

STAGE 03

One year to seven years

As kittens become adult cats, activity and nutritional needs may change. Planned meals rather than constant snacking can help you monitor intake and manage weight. The right feeding approach depends on your cat’s health, activity, and veterinarian’s guidance.

STAGE 04

Seven years and more

Older cats may experience changes in body condition and nutritional needs. The supplied guide recommends attention to quality protein and appropriate fat and calorie intake. Look for clearly identified protein sources on food labels and discuss dietary changes with your veterinarian.

Feeding information is educational and should be tailored to your cat with veterinary guidance, particularly for very young or senior cats.
