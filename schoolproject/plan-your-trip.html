Getting Here

Most visitors arrive by air. Taniti’s small airport currently accommodates small jets and propeller planes. Airport expansion is planned to accommodate larger jets in the coming years.

A small cruise ship also docks in Yellow Leaf Bay for one night per week.

Getting Around

Choose a transportation option based on where you plan to explore:

  • Public buses: Serve Taniti City daily from 5 a.m. to 11 p.m.
  • Private buses: Serve other areas of the island.
  • Taxis: Available in Taniti City.
  • Rental cars: Available from a local agency near the airport.
  • Bicycles: Available from several vendors; helmets are required by law.
  • Walking: Taniti City is fairly flat and walkable, and Merriton Landing is easy to explore on foot.

Where to Stay

Taniti offers lodging ranging from an inexpensive hostel to a large four-star resort. Visitors can also choose from small family-owned hotels and a growing number of bed and breakfasts.

All types of lodging are regulated and regularly inspected by the Tanitian government.

Food & Dining

Taniti has ten restaurants: five primarily serve local fish and rice, three offer American-style meals, and two serve Pan-Asian cuisine.

For groceries, the island has two supermarkets, two smaller grocery stores, and a convenience store open 24 hours a day.

Visitor Information

Review these practical details before your visit.

Currency & Payments

Taniti uses the U.S. dollar. Many businesses also accept euros and yen, several banks exchange currency, and many businesses accept major credit cards.

Language

Many younger Tanitians speak fluent English. English is less common in rural areas, particularly among older residents.

Electrical Outlets

Power outlets are 120 volts, the same voltage used in the United States.

Safety & Healthcare

Violent crime is very rare, but reports of pickpocketing and other petty crime have increased with tourism. Taniti has one hospital and several clinics; the hospital has many multilingual employees.

National Holidays

Taniti observes many national holidays. Attractions and restaurants may close on these days, so plan accordingly.

Local Rules

Alcohol cannot be served or sold from midnight to 9 a.m. The legal drinking age is 18. Bicycle helmets are required by law.
